## Screenshot 1: "I'm starting to think my number is blocked" (+1 672-337-6551)

### Verdict
**SCAM — 95% confidence. Playbook: Wrong-Number / Pig Butchering Opener. Target stage: Cold contact, hoping for any reply.**

My cousin, this one is the oldest new trick in the book. Area code 672 is British Columbia, Canada, but the number means nothing, they spoof these things like changing socks. The message has no name, no context, no business, no "hey is this still Sarah's number." Just guilt. Just "you ignoring me?" Because they know one thing about human beings: most of us, when somebody says "are you ignoring me," we want to say "no, wrong number, sorry." And the moment you type those four words, you confirmed the line is live, the human is polite, and you are now on the warm-leads list. Within two weeks you will be getting messages from a beautiful woman in Singapore who wants to teach you crypto trading. That is pig butchering. They fatten you up with friendship, then they slaughter your savings.

### Red flags
| Flag | Receipts |
|---|---|
| No greeting, no name, no context | Real wrong-number texts almost always include a name ("Is this Mike?"). The vagueness is the bait. |
| Two messages, escalating guilt | First "blocked?" then "ignoring me on purpose?" — classic two-step softening that real strangers don't do. |
| Sender not in contacts, generic profile | Apple is literally flagging it: "The sender is not in your contact list." |
| Area code spoof potential | 672 is BC, Canada. Cross-border SMS with no follow-up name is a known pig-butchering signature (FBI IC3 2024–2025 reports). |

### What they will send next, if you reply
"Oh sorry! I thought you were my yoga instructor 😅 you sound nice though, where are you from?" And the long con begins.

### Reply verdict
**DO NOT REPLY.** Not even "wrong number." Not even a thumbs down. Block + delete. Any response confirms the line.

## Screenshot 2: "MPM Processing — urgent legal documents" (shortcode 94202)

### Verdict
**SCAM — 99% confidence. Playbook: Fake Process Server. Target stage: Authority-pressure cold contact.**

Ah, this one I know well from my old life. We called it "the courthouse cousin." Real process servers, my friend, do not text from a shortcode. They knock on your door, with paper, in person. The whole point of legal service is that it is documented and physical. "MPM Processing" is not a real entity I can find any legitimate trace of. The 888-418-6110 number, when you search it, lights up scam reporting boards like Christmas tree.

### Red flags
| Flag | Receipts |
|---|---|
| Process servers do not text | Service of process in every US state requires physical delivery or certified mail. Texts have no legal standing. |
| Shortcode 94202 used for mass campaigns | Shortcodes are bulk SMS infrastructure, not legal infrastructure. |
| Vague "urgent legal matter" with no case number, no court, no jurisdiction | Real legal notices identify the court, the case, the plaintiff. This identifies nothing. |
| "Verification department" is fake terminology | No court or law firm has a "verification department." This is call-center language. |
| 888-418-6110 appears on scam-tracking forums | Search this number on 800notes.com and Reddit r/scams — multiple reports of fake debt / fake legal collection scams (verify yourself before believing me). |
| Generic name "John Nardone" with no context | They spray these to thousands of numbers; some land on people whose name matches, most don't. The name is bait. |

### What happens if you call
A "verification specialist" will tell you there is a lawsuit, a warrant, or a debt, and demand immediate payment via gift card, wire, or crypto to make it go away. Sometimes they ask for SSN "to verify your file." Both ends are the scam.

### Reply verdict
**DO NOT REPLY. DO NOT CALL.** If you are genuinely worried you missed something, look up your county courthouse directly (Google the court yourself, never use a number from a text) and ask if anything is filed under your name.

## Screenshot 3: "EZ Market — loan options updated" (+1 424-434-4028)

### Verdict
**SCAM — 98% confidence. Playbook: Loan Lead-Gen / Advance Fee Loan. Target stage: Re-engagement bait.**

This one is sneaky because it sounds harmless. "We have updated options for your loan request on 04/8/26." But you never made a loan request, did you? They count on you either (a) calling to say "I never applied" — confirming the line, harvesting your voice, opening a sales pitch — or (b) thinking "maybe my partner applied?" and calling to find out.

### Red flags
| Flag | Receipts |
|---|---|
| Reference to a loan request you never made | The hook. Real lenders have your name and the application number in the first message. |
| Area code 424 is Los Angeles | Random CA number for a "market" with no website domain anywhere in the message. |
| "EZ Market" is a known generic shell brand | These names rotate weekly across scam lead-gen networks. Search "EZ Market loan scam" — entries show up across BBB and Reddit complaint threads. |
| Toll-free callback to a high-pressure phone room | 1-888 numbers cost nothing to provision; the actual operation is offshore. |
| No company URL, no address, no NMLS number | Legitimate US lenders are required to disclose their NMLS license ID. Absence is a tell. |

### What happens if you call
Either an "advance fee" loan scam (pay $200–$500 "processing" to release a loan that never comes) or aggressive lead-sale where your info gets sold to twelve more debt mills who will call you for the next year.

### Reply verdict
**DO NOT REPLY. DO NOT CALL.** Block, delete.

## Screenshots 4 & 5: Fake "Maryland District Court Traffic Division" notices (+1 254-604-4091 and +1 248-772-9480)

### Verdict
**SCAM — 99% confidence. Playbook: Fake Traffic Ticket / QR Code Smishing. Target stage: Authority-pressure with payment portal trap.**

Chai, my cousin, this is the scam of 2025 and 2026. They are EVERYWHERE. The fake DMV / fake court / fake toll authority texts with a QR code are the single most reported SMS scam in America right now (FBI IC3 issued a public advisory in April 2024 and another in early 2025, FTC has issued multiple alerts). And look, both of your screenshots use the **same case number** (26-TR-273196) sent from **two different numbers** weeks apart, in two different visual templates. That is the same operation rotating numbers to evade carrier blocks. Dead giveaway.

### Red flags
| Flag | Receipts |
|---|---|
| Maryland courts DO NOT send SMS notices with attachments | Maryland Judiciary's official position: court notices come by mail. Verify at mdcourts.gov. |
| QR code to "Secure Payment Portal" | This is the entire scam. The QR leads to a spoof site that harvests card numbers + CVV + ZIP. |
| Two different sender numbers, same case number | 254 area code = central Texas; 248 = Michigan. Neither has anything to do with Baltimore. They are spoofing across burner numbers. |
| Image attachment instead of a court e-filing portal link | Real e-courts (MDEC in Maryland) require login through an authenticated portal, not a JPEG. |
| Visual escalation between drafts | Notice how the second one (April 29) gets MORE red, MORE bold, MORE "FINAL." That is template iteration by the scam crew to test which scares more people into scanning. |
| Random "Judge Michael Rodriguez" with no Maryland bar verification | Cross-check on Maryland Judiciary's judges list — name does not appear as a Baltimore City District Court traffic judge. |
| Citation of real statutes (Md. Code Transportation §21-1003, §26-204, §27-101) | This is the most dangerous part: they cite REAL Maryland statute numbers to add credibility. Scammers using real law to sell fake authority. |

### Memoir callout (professor mode)
My cousin, in the old days we used Western Union. Today's young scammers use QR codes. Same scam, new wrapper. The principle is identical: get the target into a payment flow before they have a second to think. The QR code is genius for them because (a) you can't see the URL before you scan, (b) it bypasses your phone's SMS link-filter, and (c) once it opens in your browser, it looks like any payment page. That is why your iPhone, your Android, every modern OS, now shows you the URL before opening a QR — but a stressed person scared of a court date will swipe right past that warning. Fear bypasses caution. That is the whole game.

### What happens if you scan
Either (a) credit card harvesting on a spoof Maryland Courts page, (b) malware drop targeting your mobile banking app, or (c) both. Several Maryland residents have reported losing $200–$1,500 to the "fine payment" on these scams in 2025.

### Reply verdict
**DO NOT REPLY. DO NOT SCAN THE QR. DO NOT CALL ANY NUMBER FROM THESE TEXTS.** If you have any concern about a real traffic citation, go directly to **casesearch.courts.state.md.us** in your browser (type it yourself), search by your name, and confirm. If nothing is there, nothing is there.

## Bottom line, from your retired Prince

You got five different scam crews texting you in the same screenshot session. That is not bad luck, my cousin. That means your phone number is on at least one sold scam-marketing list, probably more. The fix is not to fight each text, the fix is to:

1. **Stop confirming the line.** Never reply, never call back, never scan, never tap.
2. **Use 7726.** Every time. It is the single best thing you can do to make carriers route these to spam folders network-wide.
3. **Enable iPhone's "Filter Unknown Senders"** (Settings > Apps > Messages > Filter Unknown Senders). Puts unknown texts in a separate inbox so they stop notifying you.
4. **Assume any "urgent + pay now + click here" message is a scam** until proven otherwise by independent verification (you, typing the real URL yourself).
